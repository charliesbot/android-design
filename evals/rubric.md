# Screenshot rubric

The judge sees the renders of one case and answers each applicable item PASS or FAIL with a one-sentence reason. Judge only what is visible. When unsure, FAIL and say why.

Every case has one render per screen and view of the proposal, each named `screen/view`. A case's `surface` decides the views. Two kinds of render come on top of those:

- **Overlays:** each sheet, dialog, menu, search view, tooltip, snackbar, or picker of a screen has its own render of that screen with it open, named `screen+overlay/view`. Judge the overlay in it as part of the design: text, controls, contrast, and the Material shape of the component. Items about the whole screen (hierarchy, layout, adaptation) look at the plain `screen/view` renders, because an open overlay covers or dims what is behind it.
- **End of list:** a scrolling Wear screen is drawn at the top of its list, and again as `screen/view (end of list)`, the list scrolled to its end, so the last rows and the edge button are visible. The two renders are one screen: judge the list across both, and do not fail the top render for a row or button that only the end render shows.

- **Phone** (the previewer's Material 3 Expressive catalog): `light` and `dark` at phone size (411x923), and three larger windows in light: `foldable` (704x933, medium), `tablet` (1280x800, large), and `desktop` (1920x1080, extra large). Items about dark look at the `dark` renders; the rest look at all of them.
- **Wear** (the Wear OS catalog): `watch` (204x204, the smallest round screen) and `watch-large` (227x227, past Google's 225dp breakpoint). Both are round and dark, and the corners outside the circle are not part of the screen. Judge every item against both renders unless it names one.

## General items

R-hierarchy, R-controls, and R-text apply to every surface. R-fill, R-surfaces, R-font, R-platform, R-dark, R-adaptive, and R-idea judge phone-size cases only, and Wear cases do not list them. A Wear case judges those concerns with the Wear items below.

- **R-hierarchy:** The screen's primary goal, established by the brief, receives the strongest visual emphasis. That goal may be understanding content or performing an action. A summary may lead when understanding it is the primary task. When the primary task is acting on a list, summaries must not overshadow actionable items. FAIL when unrelated elements compete equally or supporting content obscures the primary task. The main action must remain easy to identify. On Wear, the hero may be a number or status with the actions beneath it, and the single edge button or primary button is the main action. Source: [Expressive hierarchy guidance](../sources/m3.material.io/pages/building-with-m3-expressive.md), especially prominence for important content, tasks, or actions.
- **R-fill:** At most one strong saturated fill competes for attention (a hero control, or one coherent group). FAIL when several unrelated saturated fills or cards compete.
- **R-surfaces:** Surfaces establish a clear backdrop and grouping; accents emphasize content, actions, or state. Calm, near-neutral surfaces are the default, not a requirement for every screen. A coherent full-screen content-color treatment can PASS when tonal separation keeps content and controls distinct; judge the visible relationships, not the amount of tinted area. FAIL when widespread strong fills flatten the hierarchy or unrelated colored containers compete with the task. A screenshot cannot prove color extraction or token usage. Source: [content-color guidance](../sources/m3.material.io/pages/styles/color/advanced.md), especially full-screen content-color moments and visible source content.
- **R-controls:** Every action looks tappable and reads as its own control. FAIL for text-only buttons floating alone outside a group of actions, or a toolbar whose items merge into one wide pill. On Wear, a full-width button, an icon button in a button group, and the edge button each count as their own control.
- **R-font:** The hero title or number has a deliberate voice. PASS when it is Google Sans Flex with strong axis settings (heavy bold or black, extremely narrow or wide, or visibly rounded), or one bundled brand face (Fraunces, Bricolage Grotesque, Big Shoulders Display, or Doto) used at display or headline size with clear intent (its own weight, width, or other axes suited to the direction). Body and label text stays Google Sans Flex, and Google Sans Code may carry numbers and data. FAIL for a regular or medium weight at a normal or only slightly condensed width: that reads as a generic sans even when it is Google Sans Flex, and a brand face swapped in at regular weight with no intent is the same failure.
- **R-platform:** Android conventions, no iOS habits: no disclosure chevrons on list rows, no "Cancel"/"Done" text in the top bar, content drawn edge to edge.
- **R-text:** No clipped, truncated-without-reason, or overlapping text in any render shown. Layouts reflow rather than break. Content partly hidden under a floating toolbar, FAB, or pinned bar at a scrolling edge is expected, not a failure: Google says "FABs persist on the screen when content is scrolling" and that the lower right is best because it is "less likely to cover important content" ([FAB guidance](../sources/m3.material.io/pages/components/floating-action-button.md)), and the C-review check already fails content that can never scroll clear. FAIL for text drawn over other text or controls in the layout itself, text cut by its own container, or a floating element covering the screen's main content where nothing scrolls. On Wear, the curved time text and the edge button belong to the screen; content scrolling under them is expected, and content cut by the round edge belongs to R-wear-round, not here.
- **R-dark:** The dark render is fully legible: icons and text are visible against their backgrounds (no black icons on dark surfaces).
- **R-adaptive:** The layout adapts across the size classes shown: compact phone, medium foldable, large tablet, and extra large desktop. Moving up a size class reveals more (a second pane, an expanded rail, more detail) rather than only enlarging the phone layout, and navigation moves from a bar to a rail with the margins and panes the layout guidance gives. PASS needs at least one larger render to show more at once than the compact render's first screen: a second pane of content side by side (like an inbox beside the open conversation), more of a list, or more detail ("What should be revealed?"). Content the phone only reaches by scrolling counts as revealed when the larger render shows it alongside. FAIL when every larger render shows the same single view as the phone, only rearranged or enlarged (for example art beside controls with nothing added), or when a larger render is a stretched or centered phone layout, or its structure breaks (a pane overflows the window, panes overlap, or a large empty void). Judge the layout structure only: awkward text wrapping or cramped text inside an otherwise adapted pane belongs to R-text, not here. Source: [window size classes and layout](../sources/m3.material.io/pages/foundations/layout/breakpoints.md), applied through [references/layout.md](../skills/android-design/references/layout.md).
- **R-idea:** The screen has a visible design idea specific to its content, beyond a standard component-catalog layout.

## Wear items

Wear cases add these. They apply to a `wear` case only and look at both watch renders. Sources are the Wear OS design guide pages under `sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/`.

- **R-wear-round:** Nothing important is cut by the round screen edge in either render: no text, button, or value is clipped by the circle, and no control is pressed against or past the curve. Content respects the round safe area, with larger margins at the top and bottom where the circle narrows; a full-width element near the top or bottom edge that the circle shaves off FAILS. Content that scrolls and is only partly visible at the edge is expected, but a non-scrolling screen must show all of its content inside the circle. Source: [design for the round screen](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/design-for-wearables.md), [responsive margins](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/foundations/adaptive-layouts.md) ("to avoid clipping").
- **R-wear-glance:** The screen carries one task or idea that reads at a glance. FAIL for a dense grid of data, a crowd of controls (more than about three actions at once on a non-scrolling screen), or anything that reads like a spreadsheet; lists are fine when they scroll and each row is one clear item. Source: [designing for wearables](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/design-for-wearables.md) ("Test glanceability", "Don't create complex, detailed apps").
- **R-wear-hero:** The screen's key number or status is the hero: set in a Display or Numeral style (large, short, glanceable) when a number leads, or otherwise clearly the largest element. FAIL when the key value sits in small body or label text, or when a secondary line outweighs it. Body and secondary text stays readable (no tiny or low-contrast text). Source: [type scale tokens](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/typography/type-scale-tokens.md) (Display is for "highly glanceable hero information, significant metrics"; Numeral is for a few digits).
- **R-wear-sizes:** The screen works on both watches. The `watch` render is complete and uncramped on its own, and the `watch-large` render is not broken (no stretched, scaled-up, or misplaced elements) and shows at least what the small one does; it may show more content or larger targets. FAIL when the large render drops content or breaks. The same layout at the larger size is a PASS: Wear margins are percentages, so a screen that only grows is expected. Source: [designing for larger screens](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/foundations/larger-screens-optimized.md) and [adaptive layouts](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/foundations/adaptive-layouts.md) (design for the smallest screen first, add value past the breakpoint).
- **R-wear-color:** The background is black, and color is reserved for the primary action, a status, or the hero value. Secondary actions and supporting content use neutral or container roles. FAIL for a tinted or light background, or for many saturated filled buttons or accent colors competing equally. Source: [color: build from black](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/color.md) and [apply color](../sources/developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/styles/color/apply.md) (Primary for main actions, Secondary-Container for less prominent content).
